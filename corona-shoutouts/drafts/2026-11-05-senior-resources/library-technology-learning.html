Prepare questions that matter to you

Write down two or three tasks you would like to understand better. Maybe you want to locate a message you received, tell the difference between a search box and an address bar, or recognize what an app is asking permission to do. Specific examples help you explain where you need guidance.

You can describe the problem in ordinary words. If a screen changed and you cannot find something, say what you expected to see and what appeared instead. There is no need to learn technical vocabulary before asking the question that brought you to class.

Ask staff what equipment is available and whether you should bring a device, charging cable, notebook, or reading glasses. Do not assume a personal computer is required because the class involves computers. Similarly, do not buy new equipment just to attend without checking what the program actually expects.

Keep your learning and information in your hands

If someone is helping you practice, ask them to explain the step and then let you try it. Watching a fast demonstration can be useful, but doing a task yourself lets you notice exactly where a question arises. You can request another demonstration or a slower pace without apologizing.

Keep passwords, verification codes, and private account details out of class discussions, shared notes, and public screenshots. Ask an instructor to demonstrate with a sample account or a non-sensitive example when possible. You can ask about a confusing situation without handing control of your personal accounts to another participant.

For practice notes, record the task and the steps in words that make sense to you. Leave room to add a correction after trying them. A note that says what to do next is more useful than a page full of terms you cannot connect to the task.

Plan the visit before leaving home

Corona Public Library is at 650 South Main Street, Corona. Its general information number is 951-736-2381. Use the workshop or adult-education contacts for enrollment questions, and ask the library about access, room directions, or the building arrangements that matter to your visit.

Because the listed classes begin at 10 a.m., which is also the library's published normal opening time, ask where participants should wait or check in. Confirm holiday exceptions before traveling. A building's general hours and an individual program's schedule are related pieces of information, but one does not confirm the other.

If you are arranging transportation, save the class location, expected end time, and a contact number together. Allow time to gather your belongings and ask any final question before your return ride. If the format is confirmed as online, ask what connection instructions and equipment you will need instead.

A patient way to help someone you love

If you need information in a different format or language, ask the organizer what is available before enrolling. Confirming that support early can help you decide whether the session will meet your needs.

For relatives and friends offering support, ask what the learner wants to accomplish before suggesting a solution. Offer to help register, make the first call, or practice a task together, while leaving the choices with them. Familiarity with technology does not give anyone permission to rush another person.

Afterward, ask what was useful and what they would like to try next. Learning can be a shared pleasure when there is room to experiment, pause, and ask again.
