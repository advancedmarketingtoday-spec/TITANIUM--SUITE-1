# Holiday Food Help in Corona: Groceries, Turkeys & Christmas Baskets

**If your family could use help with groceries this holiday season, start with the Corona-Norco Settlement House at (951) 737-3504.** Its weekday food pantry and Christmas assistance programs are established local resources, and additional Corona-area pantries may be useful while you wait. Call before visiting to confirm current eligibility, inventory and holiday arrangements.

![Holiday food help in Corona: groceries, turkeys and Christmas baskets](./images/corona_holiday_food_help.png)

The holidays bring traditions worth looking forward to: favorite dishes, familiar faces around a table and a chance to share what we have. They can also be expensive. A family that manages its regular grocery budget carefully may suddenly be shopping for a gathering, a school celebration and several extra days when everyone is home. Help exists, and asking about it is a practical first step.

## Start here: Corona-Norco Settlement House

The Corona-Norco Settlement House serves people facing temporary emergency needs within the Corona-Norco Unified School District boundaries. Its address is **507 S. Vicentia Avenue, Corona, CA 92882**, and its phone number is **(951) 737-3504**. The organization's published food-pantry hours are **Monday through Friday, 4:00–5:30 p.m.** It describes providing bagged groceries to eligible low-income individuals and families who have access to cooking facilities. Recipients are screened, and the published program says qualifying clients may receive groceries once every 30 days.

Those details matter because a pantry visit is not the same as showing up at a public food giveaway. A call first can clarify whether your household falls within the service area, what identification or information to bring, and how the thirty-day interval works. If you have already received groceries recently, ask when you can return and whether a separate holiday program may apply.

## Christmas food baskets are a separate program

Settlement House also operates a Christmas food-basket program. Its published program description says it provides approximately **700 baskets** in a typical year to eligible families, individuals and senior citizens, with prescreening. That is a description of the program, not a guaranteed allocation or a confirmed 2026 distribution count.

The organization's holiday-drive wish list gives a useful picture of what a festive food basket may need: stuffing, canned vegetables, canned fruit, cranberry sauce, potatoes, soups, gravy, cake mixes and other meal ingredients. There is no need to assume that every basket contains a turkey or identical contents. Ask directly whether the 2026 Christmas program has an application window, how families are selected, whether pickup is scheduled and whether the pantry and Christmas programs have different qualifications.

For a household navigating work schedules or transportation, early questions can save an unnecessary trip. Write down the name of the person you speak with, the date of your call and any instructions they give you. Assistance rules can change as donations and volunteer capacity change.

## Other pantry options around Corona

Corona-Norco United Way publishes a local resource directory listing several places to ask about groceries. **Manna House Food Bank**, associated with Inland Vineyard Christian Fellowship, is listed at **935 N. McKinley Street** with Saturday grocery distribution from **9–10 a.m.** and contact number **(951) 549-1192**. **New Hope Worship Center** is listed with a Saturday pantry beginning at **10 a.m.**, phone **(951) 737-4673**. **Templo Nueva Vida** is listed at **930 E. Ontario Avenue** with a Thursday drive-up pantry from **11 a.m.–12:30 p.m.** and phone **(951) 444-0079**.

These are directory listings, not confirmations of this week's supplies or holiday opening hours. Some sites may have changed schedules. Contact the provider, especially around Thanksgiving and Christmas, and ask whether there are residency restrictions or limits on household visits.

If no nearby location can meet your needs, **dial 2-1-1** for a community resource adviser. Corona-Norco United Way points families toward the 211 resource database serving Riverside and San Bernardino counties. Describe your location, transportation limits and whether you need groceries to cook or a prepared meal to eat.

## Ask clearly about turkey assistance

A bag of pantry staples and a holiday turkey giveaway are not interchangeable. Some organizations distribute uncooked turkeys, some give vouchers, some offer a complete grocery package and others serve hot meals. If a particular option matters for your family, ask that question at the beginning of the call.

For Thanksgiving 2026, a separately confirmed **free frozen turkey drive-through** is scheduled for **Friday, November 13, 10 a.m.–1 p.m.**, at the **ONT Field parking lot, 9375 E. Riverside Drive, Ontario**. The announcement says no RSVP is needed and supplies are limited. It is outside Corona; travel and demand should be considered before planning around it.

Another regional program, Project Boon's **Eat & Be Well**, has announced meals and turkey/grocery assistance in Fontana on **November 25**. Its organizer says the event starts at **11 a.m.** at the Cypress Neighborhood Center, **8380 Cypress Avenue**. Families should check the organizer's latest distribution arrangements and availability before traveling.

## A simple call can answer five useful questions

When contacting a pantry or holiday program, try: “Hello, I live in the Corona-Norco area and I'm looking for food assistance for my household. Are you taking new clients, and what should I bring?” Then ask about service boundaries, appointment or arrival times, frequency limits, available food types and any separate Thanksgiving or Christmas applications.

If your household has dietary requirements, limited kitchen equipment or a need for delivery, mention that respectfully. Not every organization can accommodate every request, but knowing your circumstances may help staff suggest another program. Those who are looking for a cooked holiday meal rather than groceries should say so; that is a different category of assistance.

## How neighbors can help without creating extra work

Settlement House accepts nonperishable foods and holiday-program supplies. Its donation page lists a weekday **10 a.m.–2:30 p.m.** in-person donation window, which differs from its later pantry-distribution hours. Check its current needs before filling a car with groceries. Shelf-stable food, properly dated and in unopened packages, is often easier for a pantry to organize than a random mix of products.

Local schools, workplaces and neighborhood groups can make a bigger difference by coordinating a short list of requested items rather than collecting whatever is left over. Cash donations may help a charity fill gaps that canned-food drives cannot. Volunteers can also ask where help is needed with sorting, packing and distributing food.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Settlement House only for Corona residents?** Its service description refers to families and individuals within CNUSD boundaries, including the Corona-Norco community. Call for a determination based on your address.

**Is a Christmas basket automatic after a pantry visit?** No such automatic enrollment is stated. Christmas clients are prescreened; ask whether the programs require separate applications.

**Can I receive food every week?** Settlement House publishes a once-every-30-days allowance for qualifying pantry clients. Other pantries have their own rules.

**What if the giveaway is out of food?** Ask about the next distribution and call 2-1-1 for additional referrals.
