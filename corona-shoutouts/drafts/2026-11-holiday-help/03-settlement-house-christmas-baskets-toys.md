# Settlement House Christmas Baskets & Toys: How Corona Families Can Ask for Help

**Families hoping to receive Christmas food baskets or toys through the Corona-Norco Settlement House should call (951) 737-3504 early to ask about 2026 eligibility and registration.** The charity describes both programs on its official website, but does not publish a confirmed application deadline for this year's recipients in the material reviewed for this guide. Do not rely on an assumed signup date.

![Settlement House Christmas baskets and toys](./images/corona_settlement_house_christmas_baskets.png)

For generations, neighbors in Corona and Norco have found ways to make the season more generous. Some prepare meals, others collect toys or donate groceries, and still others volunteer time they might otherwise spend shopping. The Corona-Norco Settlement House brings many of those acts of kindness together through its holiday programs.

The purpose of this article is simple: help families ask the right questions and help donors offer the items that the organization actually needs. It is not a claim that every applicant will qualify or that supplies will be unlimited.

## Where to begin

The Settlement House is located at **507 S. Vicentia Avenue, Corona, CA 92882**. Its phone number is **(951) 737-3504**, and its general office hours are listed as **Monday through Friday, 10 a.m.–5:30 p.m.** For a Christmas basket or toy request, call during office hours rather than arriving without knowing the holiday program's schedule.

A helpful opening is: “Hello, I live in the Corona-Norco area and would like information about the Christmas food-basket and toy programs. Are applications open for 2026, and what is the process?” Ask whether each program needs a separate application. Then find out when applications close, whether appointments are required, what documents are needed, and where pickup will occur.

The organization's website says its services primarily support low-income households within **Corona-Norco Unified School District boundaries**. Living in or near Corona does not automatically establish eligibility. Staff can explain how their boundaries and screening rules apply to your household.

## How the Christmas food baskets work

Settlement House publishes that its holiday food-basket program has provided approximately **700 baskets** in past years for eligible low-income families, individuals and senior citizens. Recipients are **prescreened**. This is a useful measure of the program's long-running reach, not a confirmed number of 2026 baskets or a promise that every applicant will receive one.

The organization's listed food needs include **boxed stuffing, canned vegetables and fruit, cranberry sauce, soups, canned sweet potatoes, boxed potatoes, gravy and cake mixes**. Those are examples of requested donations; the final basket contents depend on what the charity collects and how it organizes distribution.

If you're applying, clarify how many people are in your household, whether children or seniors are included, and whether your address is within the service area. Ask if the charity can accommodate dietary or cooking limitations. Not every program has the flexibility to customize its baskets, but staff can tell you what options may exist.

## What the toy program provides

Settlement House also runs a holiday toy program supported by individuals, schools, organizations, businesses and other community partners. Its official program page says the organization has provided toys to roughly **3,000 children** during previous holiday seasons.

The donation guidance seeks **new, unwrapped toys for children from newborn through age 15**, with a particularly strong need for gifts for girls ages **9–15**. The seasonal holiday-drive page has a narrower toy-age line, so donors should call to confirm the current age priorities. The organization also requests new books and batteries to accompany toys that need them.

For families, the key questions are how children are registered, whether there are age limits or a household cap, and whether proof of a child's age or guardianship is needed. The website does not establish that toys are distributed to everyone who calls. Screening, capacity and timing should be confirmed directly.

## The calendar deserves an early phone call

A Christmas program often involves several behind-the-scenes stages: collecting donations, screening applicants, recruiting volunteers, sorting items and arranging pickup. That is why early contact matters even when an exact date has not yet been posted. A quick call can tell you whether your family should apply now, when to call again, or whether another organization is a better option.

Make notes during the conversation: who you spoke to, the date, requested documents and any reference or appointment information. If the family uses a shared vehicle or has limited transportation, ask whether pickup is available at a specific time rather than assuming the main office is the distribution location.

Don't send copies of private documents through unofficial social-media accounts or informal messaging groups. Use the charity's directly confirmed contact process. If a message asks for payment to “hold” a free basket or toy, call the official number to verify its legitimacy.

## The everyday food pantry is different

Settlement House also operates a weekday pantry. Its published pantry service window is **Monday–Friday, 4–5:30 p.m.** for eligible households, with groceries generally available once every 30 days to qualifying clients. This **regular pantry schedule is not the Christmas basket pickup schedule**.

If you need groceries before Christmas, tell staff that when you call. You may be able to ask about pantry eligibility separately from holiday assistance. Families who do not qualify for one program should not assume that all local food resources are closed to them. Dialing **2-1-1** may help identify alternative services based on your circumstances.

## A practical guide for neighbors who want to donate

The charity's holiday-drive page names food staples and **new, unwrapped toys** among its needs. If you're planning a school, workplace or neighborhood donation drive, contact staff first to get a current list and the last date for delivery. Too many of one item and too few of another can make packing difficult.

Settlement House lists a donation drop-off window of **10 a.m.–2:30 p.m., Monday through Friday**, at its Vicentia Avenue location. Verify that holiday-drop-off arrangements are still the same before bringing large collections. For financial giving, use the organization's official donation channel rather than responding to an unfamiliar payment request.

Volunteer groups can ask about sorting food, filling holiday boxes and wrapping gifts. The official programs page says volunteers under age 16 should be accompanied by an adult for listed holiday activities. Check whether signups or minimum age rules have changed.

## Respectful sharing makes a difference

If you know a family who might appreciate assistance, offer this phone number privately and without assumptions. Families have different budgets, obligations and comfort levels with sharing their circumstances. An invitation to explore a program is more helpful than making promises on the program's behalf.

Organizations also benefit when community members share accurate contact details instead of circulating photographs from earlier years. Even a well-intended post can create confusion if a past deadline is mistaken for the present one.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is there a confirmed 2026 signup deadline?** Not in the official program information reviewed here. Call **(951) 737-3504** and ask.

**Does the program serve Norco too?** Settlement House describes serving households in the Corona-Norco district area; staff determines eligibility.

**Can I apply for both food and toys?** Ask whether separate screening and forms are required. The published site does not guarantee combined enrollment.

**Are donations needed?** Yes. The organization lists food, toys, books, batteries and volunteer help among its holiday needs.

## A warm Christmas wish for Corona and Norco

The most meaningful part of these programs is the simple idea that neighbors deserve to feel included and supported during the holidays. Whether you are looking for help, dropping off a toy, sharing a resource or lending a hand, you are part of that spirit.

**Please confirm all dates and rules directly with Settlement House before making plans.**
