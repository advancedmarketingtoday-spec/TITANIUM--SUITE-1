# Free Thanksgiving Meals Near Corona: Where to Share a Hot Meal in November 2026

**Looking for a free Thanksgiving meal in or around Corona?** Verified 2026 options include **Project Boon's Eat & Be Well celebration in Fontana on Wednesday, November 25**, and **Serve the People's free Thanksgiving Day gathering in Santa Ana on Thursday, November 26**. Corona's Angels over Cliffs also lists a **November 20 Thanksgiving Feast**, with details still pending. Confirm admission, accessibility and availability before making the trip.

![Free Thanksgiving meals near Corona](./images/corona_free_thanksgiving_meals.png)

Thanksgiving can mean a large dinner with relatives, a quieter day with a friend, or simply enjoying a warm plate of food among neighbors. No one should feel that they need to put on a big celebration to be part of the season. Across Southern California, nonprofits and volunteers are creating opportunities to sit down, share a meal and connect.

This guide focuses on **prepared meals**, rather than turkey giveaways or Christmas grocery baskets. If you need ingredients to cook at home, see our separate holiday food-help and turkey-giveaway guides. If you need a ready-to-eat dinner, the differences matter: a frozen turkey is wonderful for a household with an oven, but not helpful to everyone.

## Wednesday, November 25: Project Boon Eat & Be Well in Fontana

Project Boon has announced its **2026 Eat & Be Well Thanksgiving** celebration for **Wednesday, November 25**, with hot-meal service described as beginning at **11 a.m.** The location listed by the organizer is the **Cypress Neighborhood Center, 8380 Cypress Avenue, Fontana, CA 92335**.

The organization describes a celebration that combines hot Thanksgiving meals with community support and a distribution of uncooked turkeys and groceries to under-resourced households. Plans include serving more than a thousand meals. Those figures describe organizer expectations, not a guarantee that a meal or package will still be available at any particular arrival time.

A volunteer-registration listing includes a broader time range of **7 a.m.–3 p.m.**; those are volunteer hours and should not be mistaken for public serving hours. Verify the attendee time directly with Project Boon before driving.

For families from Corona, Fontana may be worth considering, particularly when everyone wants a warm prepared plate. Ask whether there is a reservation process, whether an adult must accompany children, whether seating is available and whether anyone needing to-go meals must follow a different procedure. Do not assume a takeout option has been confirmed simply because hot meals are offered.

This opportunity may appeal to people who would enjoy a full community celebration instead of only picking up a meal. Still, the most useful details are the practical ones: date, time, address, registration and capacity.

## Sit-down, takeout and grocery assistance are different

“Free Thanksgiving food” can describe three different experiences. **A sit-down meal** typically means attendees eat at the venue at a scheduled time. **A to-go meal** is packaged for eating elsewhere. **A grocery distribution** provides ingredients for later cooking. An organizer may offer one, two or all three, but the article or flyer needs to say which.

Before leaving home, call or check the official page. Ask: Is the meal served hot? Do I need a reservation? Can I take a plate home? Is there a household limit? Are children and seniors welcome? Does the event require identification or proof of residency? Are there vegetarian or other dietary options? Can someone pick up a meal for an individual who cannot travel?

For a reader managing a disability, caregiving duties or limited transportation, those questions can be more important than the event's advertised size. Organizers generally appreciate clear requests for accessibility information.

## Finding a meal when the listed events do not fit

If your schedule or location makes these options impractical, call **2-1-1**, which offers referrals to community resources in Riverside and San Bernardino counties. Tell the adviser that you specifically need a **prepared meal**, the date you need it and the distance you can travel. A community resource search may identify a church dinner, local senior meal service, shelter meal or other program with its own qualifications.

The **Corona-Norco Settlement House** is another established local starting point for grocery assistance, although its food pantry should **not** be mistaken for a prepared Thanksgiving meal service. The organization is at **507 S. Vicentia Avenue**, phone **(951) 737-3504**, and publishes regular weekday pantry distribution from **4–5:30 p.m.** for qualifying clients.

Checking local assistance early also gives families time to seek transportation or adjust plans if a meal requires advance registration.

## How to share the information thoughtfully

The best Thanksgiving recommendation is one that fits a person's actual needs. Before forwarding an event to a neighbor, check whether it is a hot meal or a take-home grocery box. Include the event address and direct organizer link. If you offer someone a ride, ask how long they can comfortably wait and whether they need to bring children or mobility equipment.

For volunteers, the safest first move is to contact an event organizer through the official website. A small amount of well-coordinated help can be more useful than unexpected extra food or a last-minute arrival by a large group. Organizations may need people to set tables, package meals, welcome guests or clean up; let staff say what they need.
