Corona Heritage Park Keeps the City’s Citrus Story Within Reach

Inside the volunteer-run museum, gardens and archives at 510 West Foothill Parkway

Corona Heritage Park gives residents a free place to explore the city’s citrus roots, donated family artifacts and early twentieth-century buildings. The volunteer-run foundation cares for the museum, gardens and archives at 510 West Foothill Parkway. Visitors can help by volunteering, joining the garden group, donating or simply spending an afternoon learning.

A company store became a community museum

The Corona Heritage Foundation is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit that oversees the park. Its museum occupies the former Foothill Ranch Company Store, a 5,000-square-foot building completed in 1937. The collection now holds thousands of objects donated by current and former Corona residents since the museum opened in 2000.

The shelves and exhibit cases carry the kinds of things families save: clothing, tools, toys, photographs, documents, art and household furnishings. Some stay on permanent display, while others appear in rotating exhibits. That mix lets the museum connect the city’s public history with the personal records that neighbors chose to preserve.

The museum’s current exhibit, Leo Kroonen: Landmarks & Legacy, focuses on the Corona architect and builder whose work included the 1913 City Hall, local schools, the Municipal Plunge and private homes. The official museum page lists it as on view now; visitors should confirm exhibit availability before making a special trip.

Volunteer care keeps Corona’s citrus landscape visible

The museum is one part of a larger historic site. The Foundation says the grounds preserve five acres of the former Foothill Lemon Company base camp. The City of Corona’s nonprofit directory also notes historic homes, citrus packing buildings, botanical gardens, a specimen grove and an entomological lab at the park.

Outdoors, visitors can walk among more than 20 historic trees, a koi pond, a memorial rose garden and an organic grove with more than 60 citrus trees planted in the 1990s. These features give the museum’s citrus story a living setting. The gardens are maintained with help from the Corona Heritage Garden Society.

Longtime volunteer curator Noella Benvenuti has helped build the museum’s systems for receiving, processing and archiving donated material since 2002, according to the museum. The site says volunteers have cataloged the collection and are now digitizing photographs, work that makes local records easier to preserve and use.

Four simple ways to take part

  • Visit the museum and gardens. Regular public hours are Tuesday through Saturday, 10 a.m. to 2 p.m. Pacific, excluding holidays. Admission and parking are free. The address is 510 West Foothill Parkway, Corona, CA 92882.
  • Ask about volunteering or community service. The Foundation lists volunteer, mentorship and community-service opportunities. Community-service hours depend on program needs, so call the manager’s office at (951) 898-0687 before planning.
  • Join the garden group. The Corona Heritage Garden Society posts meetings on the third Tuesday of each month at 9 a.m. at the park. Annual membership is listed at $30; confirm the current meeting and membership details before attending.
  • Support preservation. The Foundation accepts donations for collections, grounds, restoration and educational programs through its official donation page.
