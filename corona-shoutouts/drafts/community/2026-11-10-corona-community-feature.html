Friends of the Corona Public Library Turns Donated Books Into Community Support

A volunteer-run BookShop gives Corona readers an affordable place to browse while helping the library fund books, programs and services

Inside the Corona Public Library, volunteers sort, price and sell donated books through a shop built to give back. Friends of the Corona Public Library has supported local readers since 1964, and its BookShop at 650 South Main Street offers residents a practical way to help: donate a book, buy one or volunteer.

A Corona idea that began with 25 residents

The Friends’ published history traces the group to September 28, 1964, when 25 Corona residents met to support a library then located in a Carnegie building at Eighth and Main streets. Librarian Lyle Perusse spoke with the group about the need for more books and a larger building.

The City of Corona identifies the Friends as an independent 501(c)(3) support group for the library. Its work has included the BookShop, Adopt-a-Book, summer reading, author talks, special programs and gifts to the library. That combination matters locally because residents can support several library needs through one volunteer organization.

The BookShop keeps donated reading material moving

Library trustees approached the Friends about creating a used bookstore in 1991, according to the organization’s history. The BookShop opened in 1993 and now carries current hardcovers, paperbacks, children’s books, classics, cookbooks, history titles, DVDs, CDs and audiobooks.

Donations go into the large wooden box inside the library. The Friends says library staff can first add a needed donated title to the library collection; other suitable materials are sorted and priced for resale. The organization reports that BookShop sales generate about $90,000 to $100,000 a year for library books, programs and services. That amount is self-reported by the Friends and was retrieved November 10, 2026.

Hours can change, so check the official Friends BookShop page before making a special trip.

How Corona residents can take part

The Friends is run entirely by community volunteers. Roles include serving at the BookShop desk, sorting donations, pricing materials, stocking shelves and helping with committees. The City of Corona lists Friends positions for adults 18 and older.

Prospective long-term volunteers must apply through the city, attend an orientation and pass a background check. The city says the check can take four to six weeks. Its November 16 orientation is listed as full, so residents should use the current Community Services volunteer page to watch for a later opening rather than arriving without a confirmed place.

Residents who are not ready for a volunteer commitment can still browse the shop, donate suitable books or learn about membership through the Friends website.
