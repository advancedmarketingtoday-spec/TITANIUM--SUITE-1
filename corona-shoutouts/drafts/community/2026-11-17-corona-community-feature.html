Corona Art Association Gives Local Creativity a Place to Grow

A community spotlight on the volunteer-run gallery inside the Corona Historic Civic Center

Corona Art Association gives local artists and curious neighbors a place to see original work, learn new skills and help keep a volunteer-run gallery open. Founded in 1963, the nonprofit works from Suite 145 inside the Corona Historic Civic Center, where gallery viewing is free and themed shows change every other month.

A volunteer gallery inside a Corona landmark

The City of Corona lists the Corona Art Association among its local nonprofit organizations and confirms that the group began in 1963. The association identifies itself as a 501(c)(3) community arts organization and publishes its federal tax ID, 33-0830429, on its website.

The gallery shares one room with the organization’s meetings, receptions, classes and workshops. Visitors can enter from Vicentia Avenue and use the parking lot between the childcare center and theater. The association recommends the back steps or the accessible ramp leading around to the front door.

Residents can see local art without planning a long museum trip. The gallery has free viewing, a gift shop and work in oil, acrylic, watercolor, pastel, photography, mixed media, stained glass and sculpture. Shows are rehung every other month, so a return visit can reveal a different group of artists and media.

The room is also a place to learn

Adults can attend association classes and workshops without becoming members. The current calendar lists Explorations in Painting and Drawing for Adults on Monday, November 23, 2026, from 3:30 to 5:30 p.m. Pacific.

The informal class is for adults age 18 and older. A $60 registration covers four sessions, and the association says the four dates do not have to be consecutive. Drawing and painting materials are available for students to try while member-artist and retired teacher Mary Lou Wallace provides guidance.

Members also have a Sunday Open Studio for drawing and painting. They bring their own supplies and work alongside other artists. Children’s and youth classes depend on teacher availability and funding, so families should use the association’s current events page instead of assuming a class is running.

Four useful ways to support local art

  • Visit the gallery: Published hours are Thursday from 12:30 to 4 p.m., Friday and Saturday from noon to 4 p.m., and Sunday from 12:30 to 4 p.m. Hours depend on volunteer staffing, and the gallery closes for holidays.
  • Take a class: Check the official events calendar for current dates, ages, prices and registration. Adult workshops do not require membership.
  • Join and volunteer: Membership is open to anyone interested in visual art. Current full-year dues are $50 for an individual, $80 for two people and $25 for a student. Members are expected to help with gallery staffing, committees, workshops or other association work.
  • Support an artist: Residents can view the changing shows at no charge and purchase member artwork or handmade pieces from the gallery shop.

Exhibiting members take on a specific share of the work. The association asks each artist in a bimonthly show to staff the gallery for at least four hours during the month their work is displayed, or provide a workshop or demonstration instead. That volunteer structure helps explain why visitors should confirm the day’s hours before leaving home.

Find the gallery at 815 W. Sixth Street, Suite 145, or call 951-735-3226. Before heading out, check the Corona Weather Hub. Readers following local school teams can also visit the daily Corona prep sports briefings.
