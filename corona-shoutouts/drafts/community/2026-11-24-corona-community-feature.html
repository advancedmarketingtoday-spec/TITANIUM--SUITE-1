Corona-Norco Settlement House Turns Local Donations Into Everyday Help

A Thanksgiving-week spotlight on a Corona nonprofit serving neighbors through food, clothing and emergency support

Corona-Norco Settlement House gives local families one dependable place to seek groceries, affordable clothing and short-term emergency help. This week, residents can support that work in equally practical ways: donate pantry staples, bring a new holiday toy, shop the thrift store or volunteer at the organization’s Vicentia Avenue home.

A practical safety net close to central Corona

Corona-Norco Settlement House is at 507 South Vicentia Avenue, a short distance south of Sixth Street and the Historic Civic Center area. The nonprofit serves people who live within Corona-Norco Unified School District boundaries and are facing temporary emergencies involving food, clothing, housing or health.

The City of Corona includes Settlement House in its local nonprofit directory and identifies food, clothing, hotel vouchers and rent assistance among its services. The organization’s own program page adds utility assistance, help with medical prescriptions, clothing and shoes for schoolchildren, and referrals to other agencies when another resource is a better fit.

This local scope matters. Settlement House is built around needs that can disrupt a household quickly: a grocery shortage, a utility bill, proper clothes for school or a temporary housing problem. Assistance is screened for eligibility, so residents seeking help should call 951-737-3504 before visiting and ask what documents or appointments are required.

Food, clothing and holiday support under one roof

The food pantry distributes bagged groceries Monday through Friday from 4 to 5:30 p.m. to eligible households with cooking facilities. The organization says qualified clients may receive groceries once every 30 days.

The thrift store is open Monday through Friday from 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. It sells affordable clothing and household goods to the public, while eligible residents without income may receive clothing at no cost. Shopping there is another way to support the organization because store purchases help fund its work.

Holiday support is especially visible in November and December. Settlement House says its Christmas program aims to assemble about 700 food baskets for eligible families, individuals and senior citizens, while donated toys are sorted and wrapped for about 3,000 children. Those figures come from the organization’s current program page and describe its stated annual program goals.

Current holiday requests include stuffing, canned fruit and vegetables, cranberry sauce, canned sweet potatoes, potatoes, gravy, soup and cake mixes. The toy program accepts new, unwrapped gifts for newborns through age 15, with a stated need for gifts for girls ages 9 to 15. New books and batteries are also requested.

Five useful ways Corona residents can take part

  • Bring pantry food: The current urgent-needs list includes rice, beans, canned meat and canned vegetables, along with holiday meal items.
  • Donate a new toy: Choose an unwrapped gift for a child from newborn through age 15. The organization highlights older girls as an area of particular need.
  • Shop or donate goods: Visit the thrift store on weekdays, or deliver accepted in-kind donations Monday through Friday from 10 a.m. to 2:30 p.m.
  • Volunteer: The volunteer page lists thrift-store help, donation sorting, office support, grounds care and event assistance. Volunteers under 16 must be accompanied by an adult.
  • Give directly: The official donation page provides online and check-by-mail options. Residents should use that page rather than an unofficial fundraiser.

General office hours are Monday through Friday from 10 a.m. to 5:30 p.m., but donation and pantry windows are narrower. Calling ahead is the best way to confirm holiday-week hours, current needs and eligibility before making a trip.
