# Where Corona Families Can Get Help: Food Pantries, Utility Assistance & More

![A volunteer offers a grocery bag to a caregiver and child in a sunny neighborhood.](images/01-hero.jpg)

*AI-generated editorial illustration; depicts a conceptual scene, not a specific local person, place or program.*

Corona families facing a hard month can start with the Corona-Norco Settlement House for groceries and emergency-support questions, Community Action Partnership of Riverside County for energy-bill assistance, and their actual water provider for billing options. Call before traveling: eligibility, available funding and distribution arrangements can change.

You do not need to solve every problem in one afternoon. The most useful first step is identifying what your household needs immediately—food for tonight, help with a disconnection notice, or a conversation about the next bill—and contacting the organization that handles that need.

## Start locally with the Corona-Norco Settlement House

The [Corona-Norco Settlement House](https://settlementhouse.net/) is at **507 S. Vicentia Avenue, Corona**, and its office number is **951-737-3504**. The nonprofit describes its work as temporary emergency assistance for low-income families and individuals in Corona and Norco.

Its listed programs include a food pantry distributing bagged groceries five days a week, rental and mortgage assistance, utility assistance, schoolchildren’s shoes and clothing, prescription assistance, and seasonal food baskets and toys. Those are services to ask about, rather than a promise that every program has funding when you call.

The organization also describes serving people within Corona-Norco Unified School District boundaries. Explain where you live and ask staff to confirm eligibility for the particular assistance you need. A Corona mailing address does not always tell the whole story about a service boundary.

For food, ask when groceries are distributed, whether an appointment or intake is required, and which documents to bring. Office hours and pantry pickup hours can differ, so confirm the distribution time specifically. Mention dietary restrictions or difficulty carrying groceries when arranging your visit.

If you need several kinds of help, say so during intake. A short explanation—“We need groceries this week and have a utility notice”—gives staff a clearer picture than separate calls that each describe only part of the problem.

## For electric or gas bills, ask Community Action about LIHEAP

The [Community Action Partnership of Riverside County utility-assistance program](https://capriverside.org/utility-assistance-program) administers the Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program, or **LIHEAP**. It provides one-time assistance toward eligible households’ energy bills, subject to income rules, program requirements and funding.

Start with **951-955-4900** for screening and questions. The agency also lists **951-848-7607** for appointment scheduling and links to online scheduling from its program page. Use that page for the current application rather than an older form shared in a neighborhood group.

The agency says regular LIHEAP applications require an appointment. It lists a different emergency process for disconnected services, including designated walk-in locations. Tell staff immediately if service has been disconnected or you have a disconnection notice, and confirm which process applies before driving to an office.

Prepare current energy bills and recent income records. The agency’s checklist also requests identification and other eligibility documents; read the complete checklist or ask staff what your household must submit. It specifically advises applicants to bring their own printed documents and copies.

One easily missed distinction: the same website describes **SHARE**, but that assistance is tied to the **City of Riverside**. Living in Riverside County does not by itself make a Corona household eligible for a Riverside municipal-utility program. Ask about LIHEAP and identify the company on your bill.

## For water bills, contact the provider printed on your statement

If the City of Corona supplies your water, its [Customer Care billing page](https://www.coronaca.gov/departments/utilities/customer-care/my-bill) lists **951-736-2321**. Explain the situation before a due date or scheduled shutoff and ask what payment arrangements or hardship referrals are currently available for your account.

Have the account number, service address and most recent notice ready. Ask how much must be paid, by what date, and whether an agreed arrangement changes a pending disconnection. Write down the representative’s instructions and any confirmation number.

If another agency appears on your bill, contact that provider instead. Families around Corona, including neighboring unincorporated communities, should not assume the city handles their account. The correct billing office is the one named on the statement.

An energy-assistance application does not automatically cover a water bill. Likewise, a referral does not mean a payment has been approved. Keep talking with the utility while an assistance request is being reviewed, and ask the assistance agency how approval and payment will be confirmed.

## Build a small folder before you make the rounds

Create one paper folder or secure digital folder containing the documents agencies request. Useful starting items include a photo ID, proof of address, current bills, household income records, and any urgent notice. The exact requirements depend on the program; avoid sending sensitive records to an unofficial contact.

Keep a simple call log with the organization, contact date, next step and deadline. During a stressful week, “leave a message and wait” can become hard to track. A note that says “appointment requested; call back Thursday if no response” gives you something concrete to follow.

If you cannot make the offered appointment, ask about another time, language assistance, or whether someone can help you complete intake. Mention transportation barriers early. Staff cannot necessarily change a program’s rules, but they can explain the available options and prevent an unnecessary trip.

Ask each organization whether it knows another resource for a need it cannot meet. Keep the referral specific: a name, telephone number and service to request are more useful than a general suggestion to “try somewhere else.” Verify the next organization’s arrangements before you go.

## Make the first call manageable

A practical opening is: “I live in Corona, there are four people in my household, and we need food this week. What is the next step?” For a utility problem, add the provider’s name, the amount due and the date on the notice.

You can also ask a trusted friend to sit with you while you call or help organize documents. Keep control of account passwords and private records, and use the agency’s official contact information when submitting an application.

If you are helping a neighbor, ask what support would actually help: a ride, making copies, watching the children during an appointment, or finding the correct form. Practical help works best when the person receiving it can choose what they need.

Save the Settlement House and Community Action numbers now, even if this is a comfortable month. A useful community directory is something you can quietly pass along when a friend says things have become difficult.
