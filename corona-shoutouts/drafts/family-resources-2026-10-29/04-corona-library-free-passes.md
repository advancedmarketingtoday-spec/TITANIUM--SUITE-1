# Free Passes From Your Library Card: State Parks, Museums & More

Corona Public Library lends California State Parks and Riverside County park passes through its Library of Things. Cardholders can place park passes on hold and borrow them for three weeks. Museum offers are a separate matter: do not assume a Corona card includes Discover & Go or free museum admission.

That park benefit is enough to make a library visit part of your family’s outing plan. The trick is choosing the right pass, checking the park’s rules, and remembering that a free entry benefit does not cover every possible charge.

## Start at Corona Public Library’s Library of Things

The library is at **650 S. Main Street**, and its main telephone number is **951-736-2381**. Its [Library of Things page](https://corona.aspendiscovery.org/libraryofthings) describes park passes alongside board games, learning kits, tools and other items families can borrow.

Borrowing from that collection requires a **Full Access Card**. If your household has an online account or limited card, ask staff whether it qualifies and what is needed to upgrade. Confirm any age or identification requirements that apply to the particular item you want.

Park passes have a **three-week checkout** and can be placed on hold. The collection page says renewals are unavailable because of demand, and items must be returned directly to staff during open hours. Plan the return along with the outing rather than leaving it for the day the pass is due.

Do not reserve a paid activity on the assumption that a pass will become available in time. Check the hold status first, then verify the park’s opening arrangements. A backup picnic close to home can keep the day enjoyable if the preferred pass is still checked out.

## Know what the California State Library Parks Pass covers

The [California State Library Parks Pass](https://www.parks.ca.gov/30806) covers vehicle day-use entry at more than 200 participating state park units. It is valid for one passenger vehicle with capacity for nine people or fewer, or one highway-licensed motorcycle.

It is not a universal ticket to every outdoor destination. State Parks publishes a participating-unit list, and parks operated by other governments, private agencies or concessionaires may not honor it. Match the exact destination to the official list before planning your drive.

The pass does not pay camping, boat-use, per-person admission, tours, special-event charges or other supplemental fees. If your plan includes an overnight stay or a guided activity, check those costs separately. Ask the park directly when its fee structure is unclear.

Think of the pass as one useful piece of the budget. You still need to account for transportation, food and any optional activities. A packed lunch and a simple visit can make the savings go further than adding several paid extras because admission is covered.

## The Riverside County pass is a different benefit

[RivCoParks’ Library Park Pass program](https://rivcoparks.org/library-park-pass) lists Corona Public Library among its participating libraries. It describes free admission to RivCoParks sites, nature centers and trails, with checkout handled through the participating library.

The county page says its passes are **not valid during special events or holidays**. Review the destination’s own information for additional charges and restrictions. Do not assume the county pass covers camping or an activity simply because that activity happens inside a county park.

When placing a hold, identify which pass you need. A California State Parks pass and a Riverside County parks pass are different items with different coverage. The similar names can make it easy to reserve one while planning an outing that requires the other.

For county-park questions, RivCoParks lists **800-234-7275**. The library can explain borrowing; park staff can explain admission and site conditions. Asking each organization the question it handles usually produces a faster answer.

## What about free museum tickets?

[Discover & Go](https://www.discoverandgo.org/) offers free and discounted museum and cultural-attraction tickets through participating libraries. Access depends on the library system and its rules; the existence of the program does not establish eligibility for every California library card.

During this review, Corona’s Library of Things confirmed park passes, but we did not verify museum-ticket reservations through a Corona card. Ask Corona library staff whether a museum program is currently offered before promising the children a free museum visit.

If you already belong to another participating library, use that library’s official Discover & Go portal and read its eligibility rules. Confirm the card type, age requirements, venue, valid date and number of people covered. A discounted pass is different from free admission for the whole family.

Also check a museum’s own website for its published admission policies and free days. Keep those offers separate from library benefits. A museum’s general free-admission day may be useful even when your library does not provide a pass to that venue.

## Borrow a little more than admission

Corona’s Library of Things includes a nature or bird-watching kit with binoculars and birding books. Ask about current availability and borrowing requirements if a child enjoys looking for birds or examining the world beyond the playground.

The collection also includes board games and puzzles, early-literacy kits, and gardening resources. These can support a low-cost weekend at home as well as a trip. Choose one item the family will actually use rather than turning the visit into another long checklist.

Ask staff where the item must be picked up and returned, whether a waiver applies, and what happens if pieces are missing. Check the contents together before leaving. That quick review helps avoid confusion at return time and keeps the kit useful for the next family.

## A simple plan for your next outing

First, choose the destination and confirm the correct pass. Next, place a hold and wait for the pickup notice. Once you have it, check the park’s current opening status, weather, facilities and any restrictions that could affect your visit.

Pack water, snacks, sun protection and whatever your children need for a comfortable day. Keep the outing realistic for their ages and abilities. The benefit works best when the saved admission fee makes a manageable day easier, rather than encouraging a trip that stretches everyone too far.

Finally, put the return date on your calendar and hand the pass back to library staff. Tell another Corona family what you borrowed and how it worked. Useful savings become more valuable when neighbors know where to find them; Corona Shoutouts will keep helping make those connections.
