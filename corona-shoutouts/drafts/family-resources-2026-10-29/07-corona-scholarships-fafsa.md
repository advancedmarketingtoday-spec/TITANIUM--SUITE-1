# College Money in Corona: Scholarships & Free FAFSA Help

Corona-Norco seniors planning to attend college in fall 2027 should start with the **2027–28 FAFSA**, or ask about the California Dream Act Application if appropriate. California lists **March 2, 2027**, as the state-aid priority deadline. Your school counselor and Norco College’s financial-aid office are practical local starting points.

Paying for college involves several applications and follow-up steps, but families do not have to complete everything at once. Begin with the correct aid year, create the required accounts, and keep a separate list of scholarship deadlines.

## Use the correct FAFSA for the year you will attend

[Federal Student Aid](https://studentaid.gov/h/apply-for-aid/fafsa) confirms that the **2027–28 FAFSA is available**. That is the relevant form for a typical high-school senior beginning college in fall 2027; students attending during a different period should confirm which application year they need.

FAFSA stands for Free Application for Federal Student Aid. It is used to consider students for aid that can include grants, work-study and loans, and colleges may use its information for other assistance. Completing the form does not mean every offered award is free money.

Start at the official StudentAid.gov website. Prepare your verified account login, any required contributor information, and the financial records requested by the form. Contributors should follow the official account and invitation instructions rather than share one person’s password.

If a family situation is complicated, ask for help instead of guessing an answer. The appropriate contributor and requested information can depend on circumstances. Bring the question to a qualified aid adviser, and keep private records out of public messages or shared computers’ saved files.

## Keep California deadlines separate from college deadlines

The [California Student Aid Commission](https://www.csac.ca.gov/apply) lists **March 2, 2027**, as the priority deadline for state aid. Its guidance also identifies the California Dream Act Application, or CADAA, as a route for eligible students who are not applying through FAFSA.

Ask your counselor or an aid adviser which application applies to you. Do not assume immigration circumstances, family income or a friend’s experience settle the answer. Use the commission’s current instructions and get individual guidance when needed.

The aid application and any required GPA submission are separate things to confirm. Ask the school how GPA information is handled for Cal Grant consideration and whether you need to take action. Save confirmations rather than assuming everything transferred automatically.

Colleges and scholarship programs may set earlier or additional deadlines. Build a calendar for each school, the state priority date, scholarship applications and requested follow-up documents. The federal application’s availability is the beginning of the process, not the entire schedule.

## Find help through your school and Norco College

Start with your high school’s counseling or college-and-career office. Corona, Centennial, Santiago, Norco and Roosevelt students should use their own school’s current communications for help-night announcements. No specific upcoming CNUSD workshop date is verified in this guide.

Ask the office: “Is there a free FAFSA or CADAA completion session for the class of 2027, and what should we bring?” Confirm the date, campus, language support, registration requirement and whether contributors need to attend. An old flyer can describe a useful program without providing a usable appointment.

[Norco College Student Financial Services](https://www.norcocollege.edu/services/enrollment/sfs/index.html) lists **951-372-7009** and **studentfinancialservices@norcocollege.edu**. The office is in the Student Services Building. Ask about current assistance arrangements and whether the service fits a prospective student’s circumstances before traveling.

Norco College’s FAFSA school code is **041761**. Add it if you want that college to receive your information, and verify the codes for every other college under consideration. An application can be submitted while a student is still deciding where to enroll.

## Put local scholarship routes on the calendar

[Norco College’s scholarship page](https://www.norcocollege.edu/services/enrollment/sfs/scholarships.html) describes the Riverside Community College District Foundation application. It lists the **2027–28 application period as October 2026–January 2027**, with high-school seniors able to apply until **April 2027**. Confirm the exact closing date in the live portal.

The college says scholarships are available to admitted students, including dual-enrolled students, continuing students and transferring students. Prospective applicants should ask how admission and student-account requirements apply before trying to complete the scholarship process.

The system includes scholarships that match applicants automatically and others requiring additional answers or documents. Completing the general application is useful, but review any extra opportunities it identifies. A match is not the same as an award notification.

The page requests at least one qualified academic or professional recommendation, with family members excluded. Ask a teacher, counselor, supervisor or other appropriate reference early. Give them the deadline, instructions and a concise account of your goals and activities.

Your high-school counselor is also the right person to ask for the current local scholarship list. Specify your graduation year and interests. This guide does not promise a scholarship simply because a local foundation or service club has offered awards in a previous year.

## Make the application stronger without making it less honest

Create a document listing activities, employment, caregiving, volunteering, responsibilities and accomplishments. Include work that may not carry a formal title. Consistently helping a family member or holding a job can provide meaningful context when the application asks about your experience.

Write scholarship responses in your own voice and follow each prompt’s word limit. Specific examples help a reviewer understand your goals better than broad claims about leadership or determination. Do not invent hardships, service hours or achievements to sound more competitive.

Keep a reusable information sheet, but adapt each response to the actual question. Save the submitted version and confirmation. If a recommendation is outstanding, check the portal and follow its reminder process with enough time for the recommender to respond.

## After submitting, watch for the next request

Review the FAFSA confirmation and follow instructions for checking your submission and correcting errors. Monitor each college’s student portal and email for requested documents. A submitted FAFSA does not automatically complete every campus’s aid file.

When comparing offers, separate grants and scholarships from loans and work-study. Look at total attendance costs, not just tuition, and ask what must be repaid or earned through employment. An aid office can explain the terms before the family makes a commitment.

Choose one task for tonight: start the right form, contact the counselor, or open the scholarship portal. Then put the next step on the calendar. Share this Corona Shoutouts guide with another senior’s family so useful local help reaches more than one household.
