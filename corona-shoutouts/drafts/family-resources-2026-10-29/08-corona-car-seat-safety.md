# Car Seat Safety in Corona: Free Checks & Installation Help

Corona families can ask the California Highway Patrol for a child-safety-seat check and help learning correct installation. Start with CHP’s office locator or the Riverside office at **951-637-8000**. Confirm an appointment, location and any cost before going; do not assume a nearby fire station offers walk-in inspections.

A useful check answers the questions specific to your child, seat and vehicle. It is worth requesting help when you buy a seat, change vehicles, move to another stage, or simply are unsure whether the current setup is right.

## Where to ask for a local appointment

The [California Office of Traffic Safety](https://www.ots.ca.gov/child-passenger-safety/) says local CHP offices conduct child-safety-seat checks. The [CHP child-safety-seat page](https://www.chp.ca.gov/programs-services/programs/child-safety-seats/) directs families to contact an area office and ask for a child passenger safety technician.

CHP’s [Riverside office listing](https://m.chp.ca.gov/office.aspx?id=840) gives **8118 Lincoln Avenue, Riverside**, and **951-637-8000**. This is a nearby contact for Corona families, not a claim that the inspection will necessarily take place at that address or be available without scheduling.

When calling, say: “I would like a child passenger safety appointment. Do you currently offer free checks, and where should we meet?” Ask how far ahead to book, how much time to allow and whether there are any requirements for the appointment.

For another option, use the inspection-station search linked from [NHTSA’s car-seat guidance](https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle-safety/car-seats-and-booster-seats). NHTSA says certified technicians generally inspect seats free of charge, and some locations offer virtual inspections. Verify the specific provider’s arrangements before making plans.

## Fire stations: ask before arriving

Some families associate installation help with the local firehouse. Availability depends on whether an agency runs a child-passenger-safety program and has a qualified technician available. Emergency-response staffing alone does not establish that a station offers this service.

This guide does **not** confirm a Corona Fire Department walk-in inspection program. If you prefer fire-department help, ask through the department’s non-emergency contact channel whether it currently provides checks or refers families elsewhere. Do not use 911 to arrange an appointment.

If a station cannot help, that does not mean you have reached a dead end. Return to CHP or the inspection locator and request the next available option. Keep the confirmed appointment details on your phone so you arrive at the correct place.

## California’s current rules by age

As of this review on **October 29, 2026**, CHP summarizes California’s child-restraint requirements as follows. These are general rules; unusual seating or vehicle circumstances should be discussed with a technician using the official guidance.

| Child’s age or size | Current general requirement described by CHP |
| --- | --- |
| Under 2 | Rear-facing car seat unless the child weighs at least 40 pounds **or** is at least 40 inches tall; follow the seat manufacturer’s limits |
| Under 8 | Car seat or booster in the back seat, subject to the statutory exceptions |
| Age 8 or at least 4 feet 9 inches | At minimum, a properly used safety belt; a booster may still be appropriate |

The exception allowing a different seating arrangement is not a reason to skip an appropriate restraint. If you are unsure how a rule applies to your vehicle or child, ask the technician rather than trying to interpret a short table as a complete legal guide.

Also distinguish a legal minimum from the safest next step for an individual child. A birthday alone does not demonstrate that an adult belt fits well. Recheck California’s official rules if you read or publish this guide in a later year.

## Bring what makes the appointment useful

Ask whether to bring the child, and take the seat’s instruction manual and the vehicle owner’s manual if available. If the printed manuals are missing, download the correct versions from the manufacturers. Make sure the manual matches the seat model and vehicle year.

Have your child’s current height, weight and age ready. Tell the technician about the usual travel routine, including another caregiver’s car, school pickup or a seat frequently moved between vehicles. A solution needs to work for the circumstances in which the seat is actually used.

Explain any concern about the seat’s condition or history. If it was used previously, inherited, or involved in a crash, say so. Bring the model information and manufacture date from the label so the technician can help identify the relevant instructions and questions.

Arrive with enough time to participate. The goal is to understand the setup and be able to repeat it, not simply leave with straps adjusted by somebody else. Ask for another demonstration if a step remains unclear.

## Ask about fit, installation and the next transition

NHTSA recommends selecting a restraint for the child’s size and age that also fits the vehicle. Follow both manufacturers’ instructions and keep the child in the current stage until the applicable limits are reached. Its guidance recommends the back seat at least through age 12.

At the appointment, ask the technician to explain the installation method, relevant attachment points, harness fit and any limits that matter for your seat. Avoid adding an accessory merely because another family uses it; ask whether the seat manufacturer permits it.

For a child approaching a booster or seat belt, request a specific explanation of readiness and fit. Have the technician show you what to check in the vehicle your child rides in. A conversation about the next stage can prevent a rushed change later.

Ask what should prompt another check: growth, a vehicle change, an installation question or another relevant event. Write the important information down with the model details. The more caregivers who understand the instructions, the easier consistent use becomes.

## Make safety part of the everyday handoff

If grandparents, babysitters or another parent provide rides, share the seat’s instructions and what you learned. Ask whether their vehicle needs its own appointment. A seat that is straightforward in one car can require a different installation approach in another.

Register the seat with its manufacturer so recall notices can reach you, and keep the instructions accessible. When anything changes, return to the manufacturer’s guidance or a qualified technician rather than relying on a remembered tip from a different seat.

Your first step can be a short call to CHP. Save the confirmed appointment and bring your questions without embarrassment; helping families understand correct use is the point of the service. Pass this Corona Shoutouts guide to another caregiver who has been meaning to get a seat checked.
