# Renting vs. Buying in Corona: Questions to Ask Before You Move

*Compare the full commitment, the property and the routine—not only the advertised payment.*

Renting and buying in Corona answer different needs. Your expected time in the area, savings, maintenance preferences and work routine all belong in the decision. There is no single financial result this guide can promise for every household.

Begin with the [cost-of-living budget](cost-of-living-corona-california.md). Put the recurring costs and one-time expenses for each actual option side by side, using written quotes and current documents.

### Questions for a rental

Ask the property manager for the complete lease terms before committing. Clarify the rent, due dates, included services, separately billed utilities, parking, pet policies and the process for maintenance requests.

Ask how move-in scheduling works. If the building has elevators, loading areas or gate access, arrange the details with management rather than assuming the moving truck can use them.

Inspect the unit you expect to occupy when possible. Note existing damage with dated photographs and request written clarification of anything that differs from the advertisement. Read the agreement carefully and seek qualified advice for legal questions; this page does not interpret lease clauses or establish deposit limits.

### Questions for a purchase

Request estimates for the specific property and financing. Include insurance, taxes, association charges if applicable and maintenance needs in addition to the mortgage payment.

The [Board of Equalization explains supplemental assessments](https://www.boe.ca.gov/proptaxes/supplemental-assessment/) after certain ownership changes and new construction. Ask your escrow, lender and county contacts how those issues may affect the property, including the timing of bills.

Get insurance information early. The [Department of Insurance’s residential guide](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/105-type/5-residential/) is an official starting point for coverage questions. A quote and the policy terms are more useful than a general claim that an area is easy or difficult to insure.

Ask the appropriate jurisdiction about permits for planned alterations. Do not assume a previous owner’s work or a future improvement is approved based only on a listing.

### Checks that apply to both

Verify the parcel and city boundary through the [City’s map resources](https://gis.coronaca.gov/server/rest/services/PropertyInformation/Administrative_Boundaries_and_Parcels/MapServer) or the responsible county office. Identify the school district, utilities and waste collection arrangement by address.

The [utility checklist](corona-utilities-moving-checklist.md) can help organize those calls. Then test the travel routine using the [commuting guide](commuting-from-corona-91-15-metrolink.md). A home can meet your space requirements while creating a daily trip you do not want.

### Make a decision with the missing information visible

Write down what you know, what you have only been told and what still needs verification. Compare the options over a time horizon you expect to remain, with help from qualified financial or transaction professionals when needed.

Return to the [moving-to-Corona guide](moving-to-corona-california.md) for the rest of the planning sequence. Before paying or signing, complete your document review and address checks so the decision rests on the home you will actually occupy.
