# California Citrus State Historic Park: Your Complete Riverside Visitor Guide

**Last updated:** October 13, 2026

## Quick Answer

California Citrus State Historic Park is a 247-acre California State Parks site in Riverside, just outside Corona, built to preserve and explain the history of Southern California's citrus industry. It sits at 9400 Dufferin Avenue, Riverside, CA 92504, and is open daily, with extended weekend hours from April through September. Visitors come for grove walks, a free museum, hilltop views, picnic spots, and seasonal guided tours.

## Key Takeaways

- **Location:** 9400 Dufferin Avenue, Riverside, CA 92504 — minutes from Corona and the 91 Freeway
- **Size:** 247 acres, including a Citrus Varietal Grove, open knolls, and lower picnic grounds
- **Hours:** Daily 8 a.m.–5 p.m. on weekdays (April–September), extended to 7 p.m. on weekends during the same season
- **Cost:** Walking the park is free; parking runs about $7 per car
- **Best for:** Families, history lovers, photographers, and anyone curious about California's "second Gold Rush"
- **Tours:** Free guided grove tours run Friday, Saturday, and Sunday at 10 a.m.
- **Dogs:** Allowed on leash in most outdoor areas — check posted rules before you go
- **Time needed:** Most visits take 1–3 hours

## What Is California Citrus State Historic Park?

California Citrus State Historic Park is a state-run historic site that tells the story of how citrus transformed Southern California's economy and landscape. It's not a theme park or a working commercial grove — it's a living museum where real orange and lemon trees double as historical artifacts.

The park includes a visitor center and museum, a network of trails through planted citrus varieties, and open grassy knolls offering panoramic views of the surrounding valley. California State Parks manages the site, with the nonprofit Friends of California Citrus Park supporting programming and fundraising.

**Think of it less like a farm tour** and more like an outdoor museum where the "exhibits" are living trees, century-old packing history, and the hills citrus growers once called gold.

## Where Is It Located, and How Do I Get There?

The park sits at 9400 Dufferin Avenue, Riverside, CA 92504, on the western edge of Riverside near Corona.

- From Corona, head east on the 91 Freeway toward Riverside, then follow signs toward Van Buren Boulevard and Dufferin Avenue.
- On-site parking is paved and marked, roughly $7 per car.
- Arrive a few minutes early on weekends; the lot can fill during event days or tour mornings.

**Common mistake:** Search for "California Citrus State Historic Park visitor center" rather than a generic address pin, since the entrance and lot are set back from the main road.

## Hours, Admission, and Is It Open Today?

The park is open daily. During the peak season (April–September), hours run 8 a.m.–5 p.m. on weekdays and extend to 7 p.m. on weekends. Outside that window, plan for the standard daily 8 a.m.–5 p.m. schedule.

- **Walking the grounds:** Free
- **Parking:** About $7 per vehicle
- **Museum access:** Included, located next to the main visitor facility

Check the official page before heading out for holiday closures or special-event schedule changes.

## What Can You Do?

- **Walk the Citrus Varietal Grove** — paths wind past labeled orange, lemon, and grapefruit varieties
- **Visit the museum** — exhibits cover packing houses, labor history, and the citrus industry's rise
- **Climb the knolls** — elevated grassy areas give wide views across the full 247-acre park
- **Join a free lecture or nature talk** — seasonal programs run through local partners
- **Photograph the groves** — golden-hour light on orange trees is a favorite for photographers

## Is It Good for Kids?

Yes — flat walking paths, open knolls for running around, and hands-on museum exhibits make it easy to navigate with strollers on most main paths. The free weekend tours keep kids engaged with a storyteller.

**Mistake to avoid:** skipping sun protection. Much of the park is open grove and knoll land with limited shade.

## Picnic Facilities

There are designated picnic areas in the lower portion of the site — a solid option for a casual lunch stop before or after exploring the groves. Bring your own food (no restaurants or snack stands onsite), and note tables are first-come, first-served.

## Best Time to Visit

The best time is a weekday morning in spring or fall, when temperatures are mild and the park is less crowded. Weekend mornings are better if you want to join a free guided tour.

## Tours and Events

- **Weekly grove tours:** Free, 10 a.m., Friday–Sunday
- **Bilingual Grove Tour:** A half-mile walking tour tied to Latino Conservation Week
- **History lectures and nature talks:** Seasonal programs through community partners
- **Citrus-themed celebrations:** Seasonal events appear on the park's partner events calendar

## Can You Bring Dogs?

Generally, leashed dogs are welcome in outdoor areas, but access can vary by trail or during special events. Keep dogs leashed at all times, bring water (shade is limited), and check posted signage at the entrance.

## The History and What It Teaches

The park preserves the story of how citrus agriculture reshaped Southern California's economy, land use, and identity in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The Citrus Varietal Grove shows multiple historic fruit varieties side by side, while the museum connects that agricultural story to labor history, packing houses, and regional growth. Citrus didn't just feed people — it built towns, created jobs, and shaped the water and rail systems still used across the Inland Empire today.

## FAQ

**Is it free to enter?**
Walking the grounds is free, but parking costs roughly $7 per vehicle.

**How far is it from Corona?**
It's a short drive, located at 9400 Dufferin Avenue, Riverside, just across the Corona city line.

**Do I need to book guided tours in advance?**
No reservation is typically required for the regular Friday–Sunday 10 a.m. grove tours, but arriving early is smart on busy weekends.

**Can I taste citrus fruit during a tour?**
Tasting isn't guaranteed — it depends on seasonal fruit availability.

**Is the park stroller-friendly?**
Yes — main grove paths are flat and wide, though some knoll areas involve a gentle incline.

**Are there restrooms?**
Yes, near the main visitor center area.

## Conclusion

California Citrus State Historic Park is one of the easiest, most affordable history outings near Corona and Riverside — free to walk, rich in local history, and manageable in an afternoon. Check current hours on the official park page, pick a Friday–Sunday morning if you want the free guided tour, and bring a few dollars for parking.
