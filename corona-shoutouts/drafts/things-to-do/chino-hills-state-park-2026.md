# Chino Hills State Park: The Complete Visitor Guide

**Last updated:** October 13, 2026

## Quick Answer

Chino Hills State Park is a 14,102-acre open space area sitting about 10 miles northwest of Corona, spread across Orange, Riverside, and San Bernardino counties. It offers more than 90 miles of trails for hiking, mountain biking, and horseback riding through grasslands and oak woodlands — and it's open daily with seasonal hours, a small entrance fee, and temporary closures after heavy rain.

## Key Takeaways

- **Size:** 14,102 acres across three counties, one of the largest open spaces in the region
- **Distance from Corona:** Roughly 10 miles northwest — an easy short drive for a day trip
- **Trails:** Over 90 miles total, mixing wide fire roads with narrower single-track
- **Hours:** 8 a.m.–5 p.m. (October–March), 8 a.m.–7 p.m. (April–September)
- **Dogs:** Allowed only on paved roads and in campgrounds, never on trails or backcountry
- **Rain closures:** All dirt trails close for at least 48 hours after more than 0.25 inches of rain
- **Fire danger:** The park also closes during Red Flag Warnings
- **Contact:** Park sector office (951) 780-6222; Discovery Center (714) 524-2471

## What Is Chino Hills State Park?

Chino Hills State Park is a large protected open space made up of rolling grassland hills, oak and walnut woodlands, and seasonal streams. California State Parks manages it specifically to preserve native plant and animal communities in a region surrounded by fast-growing cities.

At 14,102 acres, the park is big enough to feel wild even though it sits between major freeways and housing developments. For Corona residents, this means a genuine nature escape is only a short drive away.

**Choose this park if** you want quiet, open hills and long sightlines rather than dense forest or dramatic peaks.

## Where Is Chino Hills State Park Located?

The park sits about 10 miles northwest of Corona, bordered roughly by the cities of Chino Hills, Yorba Linda, and Brea. For Corona drivers, the most common approach is heading north on the 71 or west on the 91 toward the Chino Hills city limits, then following signs to the park entrance. Picking the entrance closest to your target trail matters — entering from the wrong side can add miles to your hike.

## Hiking Trails

The park has over 90 miles of combined trails, ranging from flat valley paths to steep ridge climbs. Most trails are multi-use, so hikers share fire roads with cyclists and equestrians.

| Trail type | Typical length | Surface | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Valley loop trails | 2–5 miles | Wide dirt fire road | Families, casual walkers |
| Ridge/canyon trails | 5–10 miles | Narrower dirt path | Experienced hikers, mountain bikers |
| Connector trails | Varies | Mixed | Linking into longer loops |
| Paved park roads | Short stretches | Paved | Dog walking, easy access |

**Common mistake:** underestimating how exposed the grassland trails are. There's very little shade once you leave the oak woodlands.

## Best Time to Visit

The best time is late fall through early spring, when temperatures are cooler and the grassland hills turn green after rain. Summer visits are fine if you go early morning or closer to evening.

- **November–March:** Mild temps, greener hills, occasional rain-related closures
- **April–May:** Wildflowers and green grass — often the most photogenic window
- **June–September:** Hot and dry; plan hikes before 10 a.m. or after 4 p.m.

## Entrance Fee

Chino Hills State Park charges a modest day-use entrance fee per vehicle, consistent with other California State Parks. California State Parks annual passes are typically accepted, and some entrances use self-pay stations when unstaffed — bring cash or check payment options in advance.

## How Long Does It Take?

Hiking time depends on the loop you choose:

| Hike style | Time | Distance |
|---|---|---|
| Quick valley walk | 1–2 hours | 2–3 miles |
| Half-day loop | 3–4 hours | 6–8 miles |
| Full-day trek | 5–7 hours | 10+ miles |

## Chino Hills vs Other Southern California Parks

Chino Hills stands out for its grassland-and-woodland terrain rather than the chaparral-covered peaks common elsewhere. It's flatter overall than mountain parks but larger and more connected than smaller regional preserves. Choose Chino Hills for long multi-use trails and open-sky views close to Corona; choose a mountain park if you specifically want shade and elevation.

## Camping

The park offers campground options where dogs are permitted (unlike the backcountry trails). Reserve ahead, especially spring and fall weekends.

## Wildlife

The park protects a mix of grassland and woodland wildlife:

- **Mammals:** Coyotes, mule deer, bobcats, and occasionally mountain lions
- **Birds:** Red-tailed hawks, golden eagles, and grassland songbirds
- **Reptiles:** Rattlesnakes and lizards, more active in warmer months

Stay on marked trails and watch where you step near rocky or grassy edges, especially in spring and early summer.

## Dog-Friendly Rules

Chino Hills State Park is only partly dog-friendly: dogs are welcome on **paved roads and in campgrounds** but are **not allowed on dirt trails or in the backcountry**.

- **Allowed:** Paved park roads, campground areas
- **Not allowed:** Dirt trails, fire roads used as trails, backcountry zones

## Parking and Directions

Parking is available at designated entrance lots on both the Orange County and San Bernardino County sides. Arrive early on weekends; lots fill by mid-morning in peak season.

## Closed or Open Today?

The park is generally open year-round, but it closes dirt trails and roads for at least 48 hours after rainfall over 0.25 inches, and it closes entirely during Red Flag fire warnings. **Call before you go:** park sector office (951) 780-6222 or Discovery Center (714) 524-2471.

## Trail Difficulty

- **Easy:** Flat valley loops, good for beginners and families
- **Moderate:** Rolling hills with some sustained climbs
- **Strenuous:** Longer ridge-to-canyon routes with significant elevation gain and sun exposure

## Water and Facilities

Water access on most trails is limited, so bring your own. Facilities cluster near entrances and campgrounds. The Discovery Center offers educational programming (714-524-2471).

## Quick Checklist Before You Go

- Check rain and Red Flag status by phone or the official park page
- Bring at least 2 liters of water per person
- Leave dogs in the car or campground if hiking dirt trails
- Arrive early April–September to beat the heat

## FAQ

**How far is Chino Hills State Park from Corona?**
About 10 miles northwest — roughly a 20-minute drive.

**Is there an entrance fee?**
Yes, a modest day-use fee per vehicle, consistent with other California State Parks.

**Are dogs allowed?**
Only on paved roads and in campgrounds, never on dirt trails or backcountry.

**When do trails close for rain?**
All dirt trails close for at least 48 hours after more than 0.25 inches of rain.

**Is the park open year-round?**
Generally yes, but it closes during Red Flag Warnings and after heavy rain.

## Conclusion

Chino Hills State Park gives Corona residents a genuine wild escape just minutes from home — rolling grassland, oak woodlands, and over 90 miles of trail, with the tradeoff that dirt trails close after rain and dogs stay off the backcountry paths. Check the official park page before you go, bring plenty of water, and time your visit for the cooler months when the hills turn green.
