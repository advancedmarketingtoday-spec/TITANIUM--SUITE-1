# Things to Do Corona CA: A Complete Guide for 2026

**Last updated:** October 13, 2026

## Quick Answer

The best things to do in Corona, CA mix outdoor adventure with easy indoor backup plans: zipline across Skull Canyon, hike Skyline Drive or Chino Hills State Park, tour the free Corona Heritage Park and Museum, and close the day with dinner and a beer at a downtown brewery. Families lean toward Stagecoach Park and Tom's Farms, while rainy days call for K1 Speed, an escape room, or a trampoline park.

## Key Takeaways

- **Skull Canyon Ziplines** is the top adventure pick, with four courses spanning roughly 160 acres and views toward Corona Lake.
- **Stagecoach Park** and **Tom's Farms** are the strongest family-friendly options, both open daily with free or low-cost access.
- **Corona Heritage Park & Museum** is the best free outing, open Tuesday through Saturday, 10 a.m. to 2 p.m.
- Corona sits roughly 45 to 60 minutes from Disneyland depending on traffic, making it a realistic day-trip base from Los Angeles and Orange County.
- **Chino Hills State Park**, about 10 miles northwest, offers the region's most extensive hiking, biking, and equestrian trail network.
- **Glen Ivy Hot Springs**, right next door in Temescal Valley, is the go-to relaxed spa day when you'd rather unwind than hike.
- Budget-friendly options exist for every type of visitor: free parks, free museums, and free community events most weeks.

## What Are the Best Things to Do in Corona, CA Right Now?

The best things to do depend on how active you want your day to be. A well-balanced day trip might look like this:

1. **Morning:** Hike Skyline Drive Trailhead or zipline at Skull Canyon while temperatures are still cool.
2. **Midday:** Visit Corona Heritage Park & Museum or California Citrus State Historic Park for a shaded, low-effort stop.
3. **Evening:** Head downtown for dinner and a flight at one of Corona's breweries.

## Corona CA Parks and Outdoor Activities

- **[Skull Canyon Ziplines](/a/skull-canyon-ziplines-corona)** — four zipline courses across about 160 acres, with sightlines toward Corona Lake and Cleveland National Forest.
- **[Stagecoach Park](/a/stagecoach-park-corona)** — a large, Old West-themed park with a playground, covered picnic areas, BBQ grills, and paved walking paths. Open 5 a.m. to 10 p.m. daily.
- **[Skyline Drive Trailhead](/a/skyline-drive-trailhead)** — a strenuous hiking option with long trails and canyon views. Best tackled on a cooler morning.
- **[Chino Hills State Park](/a/chino-hills-state-park)** — about 10 miles northwest of Corona, with hiking, biking, and equestrian trails through woodland and grassland terrain.
- **[Glen Ivy Hot Springs](/a/glen-ivy-hot-springs)** — a spa-style alternative for visitors who want to unwind rather than climb.

**Common mistake:** Starting a Skyline Drive hike in the afternoon. Corona summers push well into the 90s, and the trail has little shade, so a 7 or 8 a.m. start is far more comfortable.

## History, Culture, and Easy Outings

- **[Corona Heritage Park & Museum](/a/corona-heritage-park-museum)** — free admission, open Tuesday through Saturday, 10 a.m. to 2 p.m. Vintage buildings, gardens, an arts colony, and railroad displays.
- **California Citrus State Historic Park** — just outside Corona in Riverside, open daily 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., preserving the region's citrus heritage with walking paths through groves.

## Where to Eat: Best Restaurants in Downtown Corona CA

- **Stone Church Brewing** — a repurposed historic building, good for groups and a sit-down dinner.
- **Evans Brewing** — a wider food menu alongside its beer lineup.
- **Storytellers Brewery** — a smaller, neighborhood-feel spot for a quieter dinner.

## Corona CA Events and Festivals

Corona's event calendar runs heavily through fall, with community gatherings and family celebrations. Recurring highlights include the Antique & Collectibles Faire at the Heritage Park, Halloweekend at the Corona Public Library, and neighborhood trunk-or-treat trails through October.

**Edge case:** If you're visiting specifically for an event, confirm the date and time on the city's official calendar before driving in, since community event schedules can shift.

## Corona CA Nightlife and Bars

Corona's nightlife centers on its downtown breweries rather than traditional bars or clubs, making it a low-key but reliable option for an evening out. Stone Church Brewing, Evans Brewing, and Storytellers Brewery anchor most nighttime plans, with occasional comedy nights and live music through the fall.

## Best Hotels and Resorts in Corona CA

Corona has a practical, mid-range hotel scene, which suits visitors using the city as a base for regional day trips. Most travelers choose hotels near the 91 Freeway for easy access to both downtown Corona and outlying attractions like Glen Ivy Hot Springs or Chino Hills State Park.

## How Far Is Corona CA From Disneyland?

Corona sits approximately 30 to 35 miles from Disneyland in Anaheim, typically a 45-to-60-minute drive depending on traffic on the 91 Freeway. This makes Corona a realistic overnight base for visitors splitting time between Disneyland and Inland Empire attractions.

## What's the Weather Like in Corona CA?

Corona has a Mediterranean-style climate with hot, dry summers and mild winters. Summer highs often reach the 90s, making early-morning hikes the better choice, while fall and winter days in the 60s and 70s are comfortable for all-day outdoor plans.

## Things to Do Within an Hour of Corona

Corona is a strong base for exploring the broader Inland Empire and Orange County. Within roughly an hour's drive you can reach:

- **Chino Hills State Park** (~10 miles) — hiking, biking, and horseback riding
- **Glen Ivy Hot Springs** (Temescal Valley) — mineral pools and Club Mud
- **Temecula wine country** (~35 miles) — tasting rooms and vineyards
- **OC beaches** (~45 miles) — Newport and Huntington Beaches
- **Big Bear and Lake Arrowhead** (~60 miles) — mountain lakes and skiing in season
